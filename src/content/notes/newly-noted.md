I've been browsing[^1] IndieWeb concepts and while not necessarily agreeing with everything, I like the idea of [owning your data](https://indieweb.org/own_your_data) and its associated methodology, POSSE ('Publish on your Own Site, Syndicate Elsewhere').

[^1]: -"Do you need any help?" -"No, thank you, I'm just browsing."

Meanwhile, I intend to gather content I've enjoyed posting 'elsewhere', mainly on the website [formerly known as Twitter](https://x.com/arielmessing). And in what is occasionally termed [PESOS](/tags/pesos) ('Publish Elsewhere, Syndicate on your Own Site'), list it here: a sort of Zettelkasten.

While on the subject of terminology: It is worth noting that the [official definition™](https://indieweb.org/note) (how _official_ can an indie term be?) considers notes a synonym for microblogging content -- "typically short [and] unstructured plain text" -- as opposed to an _article_[^2].

[^2]: What used to be called in the not-too-far history a _blog post_.

Rather, the way I use it should carry the connotation of a **field note**.

A note for me is formatted. It comes with a title[^3] and a description, (many) footnotes, quotes, potentially images (still need to rethink my design around it), and definitely some [executable code](/notes/i-got-a-brand-new-kitchen-timer) sketches.

[^3]: I treasure the art of newspaper-style bad puns in note titles.

And it can still fit within a single laptop screen.
