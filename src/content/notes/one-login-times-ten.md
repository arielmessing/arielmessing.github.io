I might have hinted at a recent change in our correspondence address (in the real world; here it is still [the same](/postbox)). That involved propagation to some of the most tech-savvy yet byzantine governmental units I've had the pleasure to avoid: His Most Serene Majesty's Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency.

Enter GOV.UK [One Login](https://www.sign-in.service.gov.uk/): the shiny new attempt to unite all sprawling digital services under a single, foolproof account. It promised a seamless experience, which is exactly why I immediately braced myself for trouble.

The system figured out immediately that I was using my laptop, rather than a more obvious device[^1]. "Don't worry," it reassured, "you won't have to change devices" -- and prompted me to download its identification app to the phone I was actively eschewing.
[^1]: It did so by applying social engineering in addition to technical superiority, and just asked me directly.

App installed, I was put on the spot to decide what form of photo ID it would use ("you won't be able to change this later"). I picked my passport: partly because I was curious to see if my phone would be able to scan its biometric chip (my old one couldn't), but mainly because the only other relevant option -- a UK driving licence -- is the exact form of ID _I was trying to replace_. This circular dependency felt potentially too Kafkaesque. 

Passport photo page scanned and biometric data slurped, the app proceeded to record a video of me trying not to wince when a series of flashing coloured lights were burning my retina. Too far! Move closer. Too bright! Go somewhere with mood lighting.

This done, the app sent me back to the loading animation on my computer screen ("It might take a few minutes"). Success!

My identity proven, I moved to the next step which is, of course, to prove my identity. Again, I was given a choice. I could prove it online using the exact driving licence I chose not to use before, or verify who I am in person at the Post Office ("Don't do it at the Post Office; it will take longer, and might be pricey. Go on, it's 2026, do it online!").

Easy choice.

My details submitted (do I have a second name? Not 100% sure now), including the postcode explicitly printed on the card, I moved to the next screen which enquires about my address. My **current** address. The address at which I reside _right now_ pop-your-head-through-a-window-and-check. "You won't be able to change this later."

**London, baby!**

"Have you been living elsewhere in the last 3 months?" Of course I have, I extended generously, and here's the address.

"We are now going to check your information by making some calls to an external service. Ready for it? Here it goe--" **NOPE**.

"Sorry, we couldn't verify your identity."

"Sorry, we can't tell you what went wrong because security."

Nothing to do but to make another attempt on a different device, or contact support ("We're closed. You'll get a reply within 2 working days"). I've tried the first, then the second, then went to bed.

Tossing and turning with an unexpected cold in the middle of this heatwave, an understanding dawned on me -- a few minutes before the astronomical one. They didn't want my new address. They didn't even know it. It's the old one they need (how am I supposed to explain to technical support?), and this is what I tried first thing the next morning. Success!

"Now we are going to ask you a series of questions that **only you** will know the answer to. Better gather your financial documents and have them at hand because who knows what we'll ask. Ready for it? Here it comes."

Question number one, super easy answer--  **NOPE**.

"Sorry, we couldn't verify your identity."

They left me no choice. [Post Office ID verification](https://www.postoffice.co.uk/identity/in-branch-verification-service) it is.

Did you know that 99.9% of Britons (can't remember the exact statistic) live within 10 miles of a Post Office? I didn't, and also didn't have to travel far. 

The lady behind the counter gave me a look of profound, multi-generational resignation. She disappeared into the back before emerging with the sacred Identity Tablet™. A tense flurry of tapping followed, accompanied by deeply furrowed eyebrows. Clearly this was not a common service. 

"Just follow the instructions on the screen," reassured the Master Postman, hovering around the next window, who appeared determined to keep this _someone else's problem_. 

The QR code on my introduction letter was scanned. More furious tapping. My passport was scanned. And again. "Can you hold it open for me?". And again. And again. Then she took an aggressively unflattering photo of me ("Happy with this?") against the majestic backdrop of the stationery cupboard. We're Done.

I arrived back home to find the result of my identity check, and it was as cryptic as one could expect:

>I weld canlyniad eich gwiriad hunaniaeth, mae angen i chi fewngofnodi i'ch GOV.UK One Login

Sorry! Sorry, I copied the Welsh translation they attached to the message (why?). But the original English is just as much cloak-and-dagger:

>To view the result of your identity check, you need to sign in to your GOV.UK One Login
> 
>Then you'll be sent to the service you need to use.
> 
>After you continue, you'll be able to view the result of your identity check.
> 
>This email will self-destruct in 5 seconds.

I might have just imagined the last bit. 

Log in to the DVLA website → create account → view and manage licence details → change address.

"An unexpected error has occurred, please try again later." 

**Fuming**.

## Postscript
I gave it a couple of hours, then tried again. 

One Login has definitely given up on me: in this futile attempt, the confirmation code (valid for 15 minutes) sent to my phone has arrived more than 40 minutes later. 

>"You can only ask for a code 5 times, after that the police might knock on your door."

My messages history already has more than 10.

**Tap, tap, tap.**

## ...Two Days Later...
To keep up with the spirit of Kafka, I open my inbox this morning to find an email from K. at the GOV.UK One Login Support Team. 

No new information provided, just telling me again they cannot give any more details about why my identity could not be proven. Instead, they send me to contact the DVLA directly, and even bother to warn that "[at] peak times you may experience long waits to get through to an advisor". 

Now why would I? The issue is clearly with the wording of **their own website**. They don't want my _current_ address, they want the address printed on my ID -- just like they made it clear in another screen, when they asked for my postcode.

I might email K. back with some helpful suggestion, but then again, who's that I'm hearing right now climbing up the stairs?
