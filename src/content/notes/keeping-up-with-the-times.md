Recently we took a leap and moved from the Kentish countryside, which has been our home for the better (and worse) part of the last decade, to a leafy North London suburb. 

This dramatic change of scenery brought with it a wide range of new and exciting experiences (bakeries and greengrocers everywhere, with some of the best apricots I've tasted in a **long** while). It also exposed a curious realisation regarding our new flat:

It has no clock.

(I am telling a lie. There is a clock, of sorts, on the gas boiler panel in the kitchen, frozen on the foreboding time of 18:36. It is one of those pins-in-pins-out 24h timers that, based on deep intuition, I strongly believe controls the central heating. But as the temperatures outside hover around 30°C and a third heat wave is looming, I am mentally blocking any association of radiators.)

By comparison, our old flat -- in an old converted psychiatric hospital (seriously, the irony is not lost) -- had an ample supply:

A bright orange digital oven timer (very useful for timing my tea steeping).

A central heating/hot water console, mostly hidden behind the mixer, and

A large, round, wall-mounted analogue clock. It was hanging high up in the stratosphere (that flat had some very high ceilings) above the kitchen cupboards right until our first summer there ended, the clock hands needed a twirl backwards, and the nail fell like the leaves of the row of [Japanese maple](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/acer/japanese-maples) outside the window of my old study.

All three (3!) clocks -- on a single wall, mind you -- showed roughly the same time.[^1]

[^1]: Which was 1 minute ahead of the real time: works like a charm for ensuring we didn't arrive at the station -- approx. 24 minutes walk downhill -- only to see the hourly train to London Victoria puffing smoke and slowly departing toward the horizon.

We've found a lady nearby who was happy to part with her VIKIS digital clock, for the reasonable sum of £1 sterling. This IKEA second-hand triangular gizmo came with a manual in English *und Deutsch*, home-printed on the other side of a 'Mental Arithmetic 4 Answers, Section 3 | Test 4'.[^2] It has blurred lighting and (optionally) a small, annoying chime (BEEP!) every hour (why?).

[^2]: Question: "C. 3. A letter was posted in Australia on 24 October and delivered in Britain on 4 November. For how many days was it in the post? Include the day of posting." Answer: 12 days. Twelve days from the antipodes to Blighty? Via the [Suez Canal](phil-vs-the-pathogens)? When did Royal Mail ever move that fast?!

This is great, but it doesn't help me tell when my English Breakfast brew is ready for a dash of milk.

Kitchen timers, beyond their awkward interface noted [elsewhere](https://grumpy.website/1219), tend to be accompanied by -- you guessed it -- a small, annoying chime (BEEP!) on every click (why?). Considering my default interval when baking bread is 21 minutes,[^3] that's a lot of beeps.

[^3]: Don't let the moisture escape!

The search continues. And until a better option reveals itself, I’ll be standing in the kitchen, staring intently at my mug, counting to two hundred and forty (240) in my head.
